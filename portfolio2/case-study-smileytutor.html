Refugee-Camp EdTech · Multi-Country

SmileyTutor Multi-Site EdTech & Refugee-Camp Digitisation Rollout

Location: Kakuma Refugee Complex & Kalobeyei Settlement (Turkana County, Kenya), plus Tanzania & Uganda Role: Program Manager — Education & Community Impact, Smiley Charity Organization

Theory of Change

Goal: To improve foundational literacy, numeracy, and math proficiency among underserved and refugee youth through localised EdTech interventions and library digitisations in high-need refugee environments.

Intervention Pathway: If we co-design community library digitisations and deploy gamified digital learning tools (SmileyTutor) across 30+ sites — specifically targeting 11 sites within the Kakuma refugee complex and Kalobeyei settlement — while actively engaging refugee community coordinators and families under a strict M&E progress-monitoring framework, then refugee students will overcome traditional learning barriers and demonstrate measurable academic improvement.

Situation

Rapid geopolitical displacement, restricted mobility, and resource-scarce environments in Kakuma and Kalobeyei left hundreds of refugee children without reliable access to quality math and literacy education or digital learning spaces.

Task

Direct country-level rollout of SmileyTutor across 30+ digitised community libraries and specifically oversee education programming across 11 sites within the Kakuma refugee complex and Kalobeyei settlement.

Action
  • Refugee-Camp Programming Execution: Led targeted education programming across 11 sites in Kakuma and Kalobeyei, extending platform access to 400+ refugee learners through customised orientation and peer-support structures.
  • Data & Quality Assurance: Led virtual coordination and robust M&E quality assurance across programme sites in 11 countries, actively flagging implementation gaps to leadership for data-driven course correction.
  • Intensive Stakeholder Engagement: Supervised a multi-site team of facilitators and refugee community coordinators, delivering standardised training on foundational literacy, numeracy instruction, and platform operation.
  • Incentive Ecosystem Management: Managed the SmileyCoin incentive ecosystem within fixed budget allocations, sustaining 3,500+ learners (including refugee youth) and improving tool uptime by 30%.

Learnings & Sustainability

Specialised refugee-camp programming in Kakuma and Kalobeyei proves that combining community library digitisations with localised coordinator training bridges the educational divide in displaced contexts more effectively than traditional top-down software distribution.
